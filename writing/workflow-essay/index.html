Essay on operations

You’d never launch an ad without a plan.

So why does your team switch tools without one?

Posted on X, 2026. 1 min read.

A short piece on change management inside a creative team: why new tools need a plan before the switch, and a thank-you to the people who quietly build the structure everyone else stands on.

Some thoughts on disorganization and dissonance in a creative strategy workspace:

When everyone asks for structure and nobody owns it, someone eventually just builds the thing.

Folders, stages, handoffs, the whole flow from idea to revision to submission. That person is usually the last one asked when the plan changes.

Change isn't the problem though. Unplanned change is.

A new tool can absolutely be the right call. But the plan for what it replaces HAS to exist before the switch, not figured out in the mess afterward.

"It has everything." 'Has' is a loaded word. Having a feature and having it set up for how your team actually works are two very different things.

"We should always adapt to something better." Sure. But for who?
And when you're a few weeks in, has anything had enough time to show what better even looks like?

Redoing the work isn't the hard part. Work I can do.

The hard part is not knowing whether it's building towards something or just keeping us spinning in place.

I'll own my part too. Something I'm practicing is separating my personal grievances from the actual business considerations.

Frustration is information. It isn't an argument.

However, we'd kill an ad with no data in a heartbeat.

Our own workflows deserve the same standard:

A hypothesis,

A cost-benefit analysis beyond the vibes,

And a plan before anyone flips the switch.

Adapting is easy. Agreeing on why we're adapting is the part we need to get better at.

To everyone quietly building the structures everyone else stands on:

I see you.

I salute you.

And to strategists everywhere:

Would you sign off on your team's workflow if it landed on your desk as a brief?
