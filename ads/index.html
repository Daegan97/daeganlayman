    Ads decay. Briefs fix it.

    Every account hits the same wall. More creative helps only if every new ad starts from a sharper idea than the last.

    • FatigueWinners burn out in weeks, and the backlog of “more of the same” doesn’t replace them.
    • Vague briefsEditors wait on answers, guess at copy, and cuts bounce back in review.
    • Generic ideasFeature explainers over footage. No story, no tension, nothing that stops the thumb.

    How I brief

    A brief is a tool for someone else’s hands, so it’s built for the edit bay.

    • Links firstSource, footage and assets on page one.
    • One lineThe whole story in one sentence.
    • One script tableTime, footage, on-screen and voiceover per row.
    • Pre-cut A, B, CClips named to match the table.
    • Voiceover and captionsGenerated, timed, dropped in at 0:00.
    • Flags in writingWhat to cover or never say, and why.

    Five stages of awareness

    Eugene Schwartz’s stages decide the hook and where the offer goes.

    StageWhat the ad doesExample
    UnawareLeads with a feeling. The product shows up late.Someone who sold their childhood binder opens one pack on their phone.
    Problem awareNames the frustration, then shows the way out.Sold-out shelves, told through one collector’s empty-handed weekend.
    Solution awareExplains how it works in one sentence and three steps.“Pick a pack, rip it, keep it or sell it back.”
    Product awareRemoves the last doubt with proof.A group chat that calls it fake, then watches the live reveal.
    Most awareGets out of the way. The offer is the headline.The free-pack code in the first second of Slow and Steady.

    Theory, applied

    I keep an 85-theory working binder. These shape most briefs.

    • Narrative transportationOne hero, one want, cause and effect. Absorbed viewers argue back less.
    • Peak-end ruleThe reveal is the peak, with the brand on screen. The offer is the second peak.
    • Curiosity gapHooks open a specific, closeable question and pay it off inside the ad.
    • Pattern interruptWhen the feed is loud, the quiet ad stops the thumb.
    • Awareness and sophisticationThe market has heard the big claims, so the angle has to be new, not louder.
    • Means-end ladderingFrom feature to benefit to the value it serves.
    • Social proofReviews, reactions and real pulls beat our own claims.
    • One-variable testingEach pair changes one thing, so the result means something.

    How a brief gets made

    The same four steps every time, so quality holds at volume.

    1. ResearchReviews, Reddit, ad libraries and your own numbers.
    2. AngleOne idea, one persona, one awareness stage, one offer.
    3. BriefStory sentence, script table, pre-cut clips, voiceover and captions.
    4. IterateRead results at 3 and 7 days. Change one variable.

    Tools: Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT Image, Seedance, ElevenLabs, Suno, ffmpeg, Gethookd, Meta Ads Library, Notion, Frame.io.

    Running the team’s systems

    I own and maintain the shared Drive our creative team works from: about 1,900 files, from onboarding to templates to assets. I also run the daily work behind it from one planning board that directs eight standing Claude sessions. Built with Claude under my direction, on top of decisions by my manager and team.

    • One source of truthRules and offers live in one dated log and one data file, so nobody works from an old version.
    • Sync at the start of every sessionEach teammate’s Claude reads those files, reports what changed, and updates the tools that depend on them.
    • Checks before reviewFinished ads get an automatic pass or fix report, with a timestamp and a one-line fix for each problem.
    • A person signs offNothing reaches the shared Drive until I’ve read the changes and approved them. Each system has one writer.
    • A plan for sudden changesWhen a rule changes mid-week, one playbook carries it through assets, checks, tools and briefs.
    • Shorter onboardingAfter team feedback, I cut the guide from 14 sections to 7 and added a three-page start doc.
    • Team guidanceA new-hire guide, an editor SOP, an offers guide and a dated rule log, so every change is written down with the date and the reason.
    • Briefs fitted to the editorExperienced editors get the full story brief; newer editors get numbered builds with every clip pre-cut. Same bar, less back-and-forth.
    • Tools I shared with the teamClaude Code tools for research with cited reports, realistic animated text threads, and indexed libraries of app screens and reaction clips.
