The MS Paint static

The team’s statics were polished, glossy and easy to recognize as ads. I made one that looked like someone drew it in five minutes. It became my top-spending static.

Format
1:1 static
Lifetime spend
$10K+
Offer
A free pack for downloading
Awareness stage
Solution / product aware
My role
Concept, layout, copy, build
Goal
Aim for lowest CPI

The problem

Our early statics looked like everyone else's in the category: dark gradients, glowing slabs, a big price tag. Viewers have learned to recognize that look as an ad and scroll past it. We needed a static that read as a person talking, while still putting the offer front and center.

The insight

  • Native beats polished. In our customer research and in the longest-running ads in the category, raw screen recordings and plain, honest messages outlasted slick creative.
  • Borrow a proven shape from outside the category. A hand-drawn "You | We" T-chart from another industry had been running for months. Two columns explain a deal in two seconds: what you do, what we give you.
  • The deal is the headline. Lowest-awareness viewers need the exchange spelled out, not implied.

The brief

For statics I brief myself, so the brief is a short spec rather than a script table:

Static, "MS Paint"1:1, 1080×1080
ElementSpec
FrameA real Windows Paint window: toolbar, palette, menu bar. Nothing that looks designed.
LayoutHand-drawn T-chart. Left "You", right "We". One line each.
HeadlineThe exchange itself: "Download the app" | "Give you a FREE pack". The only color on the page is the word FREE.
ProofTwo real pack images, drawn-on arrow, "= first rip on us".
BrandThe exact logo on a dark chip, bottom right. No other brand marks.
RulesOne offer only. Real assets only. Passes the banned-word list.

Iterations

The format became a template. Each version changes one thing so the team can read what moved the numbers.

Why it worked

  • It doesn't look like an ad, so it gets the second look that polished statics lose.
  • Two columns, one deal. The least-informed viewer understands the exchange instantly.
  • One loud word. Color is spent on FREE and nowhere else, so the offer is the hero.

Ideas behind it: Pattern interrupt, Processing fluency, Reciprocity, Lowest awareness, One-variable testing.
